## INTENT
A `snodo serve` running over stdio exits within a few seconds of its client going away: stdin reaches EOF, or the parent or SSH session hangs up. This holds even when a tool call (for example recon) still has background work running. Jobs that were dispatched as their own detached processes keep running.

## THE CHAIN
- Evidence: two stale stdio servers for one project ran side by side for two days on the maintainer's GPU host (launched through `ssh -T … exec snodo serve`). On macOS, the Claude desktop app had a stale duplicate stdio pair for one project. All had to be killed by hand.
- Recons rec_c5e550 and rec_d74df8 found the following:
  - `_run_server` in `snodo/cli/commands/serve_cmd.py` runs the stdio transport (around line 376) inside a try/finally that arms and disarms cloud liveness. Construction starts no threads.
  - `ReconManager.submit` in `packages/snodo-mcp/src/snodo/recon/__init__.py` (around lines 1112–1119) starts a non-daemon `Thread`.
  - `asyncio.to_thread` in slow tool handlers creates non-daemon executor threads.
  - No SIGHUP handling exists in stdio mode.
  - No test runs a real stdio server to EOF.

## CONSTRAINTS
- After the stdio transport ends (EOF, SIGHUP, or SIGTERM), the real server process shuts down within a bounded time. Detached job and monitor subprocesses are left alone.
- In-process background work must not keep the process alive.
- The HTTP/tunnel transport keeps its current behaviour.
